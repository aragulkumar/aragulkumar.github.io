why i love backend systems over frontend

feb 14, 2026

430 words · 3 minute read

this isn't a hot take — frontend is hard and i respect people who do it well. but every time i sit down to build something, i gravitate toward the backend. here's why.

backend work is mostly about logic and systems. data flows in, gets processed, stored, or forwarded — and the correctness of that process is what you're optimizing for. there's no ambiguity about whether it worked. either the right data came out or it didn't.

frontend feels subjective to me in a way that makes me uncomfortable. "does this look good?" depends on who's asking. "is this api returning the right response?" does not. i like problems with clear answers.

i also love the infrastructure side of it — how systems scale, how databases are structured, how you handle failures gracefully. these are hard, interesting problems. i could think about system design all day.

none of this means i avoid the frontend entirely. building this site, for instance, forced me to care about html structure and basic css — and i actually enjoyed it. but if i had to pick one side to specialise in, it's the backend every time.
